# Humans log in through OIDC, agents are bound by their principal's passkey, and Forge sync fails closed

Status: accepted. Amends RFC 0001 §5.1 and §8; supplies the identity rules that ADR 0008 depends on.

RFC 0001 deferred OIDC to after v1, and every Member today holds a token an Admin issued and pasted. ADR 0008 makes Team membership a visibility boundary, and the web GUI needs a login. Both need an answer to who a person is, how they reach the CLI and their agents, and how their Forge accounts and Teams stay true. The building blocks and their costs are in the [research note](https://github.com/randax/yggdrasil/blob/ba63df882d7cab45a9a40d975e801d84e5169d6e/docs/research/identity-login-team-sync.md).

Three ideas run through the rules below:

- **Authority comes from a stepped-up human act.** A Forge, an IdP or a config file may *inform* who someone is and what Teams they are in. None of them can create a Yggdrasil role, and every widening by a Member, whoever performs it, needs step-up, a reason, audit and a notice (ADR 0008).
- **The factor never reaches the agent.** Every ceremony that hands out or uses a credential is a passkey touch on Yggdrasil's own web origin, on a page that says what is being approved.
- **Freshness is stated and fails closed.** Anything derived from a Forge or an IdP has a maximum age. When it cannot be reconfirmed, it lapses and access narrows.

## The rules

### Login

- A human logs in through a **Login Provider**: a generic OIDC issuer (a corporate IdP, GitLab or Forgejo) or GitHub's OAuth, which has no ID token and is served by a separate adapter. An Admin chooses which configured providers may be used for login. A deployment may allow only its IdP and keep Forges for linking alone.
- A login identity is keyed by `(issuer, sub)`. Entra is keyed by `(tid, oid)`, because its `sub` changes per application. GitHub is keyed by its numeric user id. An email or a login name is never a key.
- Several issuers make Yggdrasil a client of several authorization servers, so every flow uses PKCE, exact redirect URIs and mix-up defence (RFC 9700).

### Who becomes a Member

- An Admin, acting with step-up, writes **Member Admission Rules**. A rule matches one of:
  - a group asserted by a login-allowed IdP;
  - an email domain asserted as verified by a login-allowed IdP (Google's `hd` claim, never its request parameter);
  - active membership of a connected Forge Namespace, checked through the Forge connection's service credential.
- An account that a Forge or IdP marks as a bot or machine account is never admitted as a human, by rule or by approval.
- A login that matches a rule becomes a Member at once and enrols a passkey before doing anything else (see *Enrolling a passkey* below). A login that matches no rule is either refused or becomes **pending**, as an Admin chooses per provider. A pending login grants nothing, Admins get a digest of pending logins on a channel separate from widening notices, and it expires after 14 days unless an Admin approves it with step-up. An approval is a standing admission record bound to that login identity. It holds on later logins until an Admin revokes it or the expiry the Admin may set on it passes.
- A human is a Member while at least one admission holds: a rule that matches, an approval, or the bootstrap naming of the first Admin. The bootstrap naming is an identity-bound admission record like an approval, and an Admin can revoke it the same way. When none holds, the Member is suspended, and so is every Personal Agent bound to them.
- The pending queue is capped, and its growth is rate-limited per provider and per source address.
- Admission is held per login identity. Every admission (a rule, an approval or the bootstrap naming) holds only while the login identity it is bound to has itself logged in within the Liveness Window, and for a rule based on IdP claims, with claims that still match. A login through another identity refreshes none of them. A Forge Namespace rule additionally needs sync to reconfirm the account.
- A Member may hold several login identities. Attaching another needs a step-up by the Member and a fresh authentication at the new provider (as for enrolment), or an Admin act with step-up. Either is audited and notifies the Member. An identity an Admin attached cannot log in or redeem a recovery link until the Member confirms it from an identity they verified themselves.
- A rule never grants the promote right beyond RFC 0001's default for human Members, never grants the Admin role, and may only use a login-allowed provider. A rule that would match any account of a provider (all of GitHub, every Google account) is rejected.
- Every login re-checks the rules against fresh claims. A Member admitted through a Forge Namespace rule is reconfirmed by Forge sync, under the freshness rules below.

Admitting a Member is not a per-repo widening for Open repos: an Open repo was already made visible to every Member, present and future, by the decision that made it Open, which ADR 0008 treats as the widening. Reactivating a suspended Member is different, because their Teams may still hold Restricted grants: the approval or rule that reactivates them needs a reason, and the Restricted repos the reactivation makes reachable get ADR 0008 widening notices, attributed to that approval or rule. This amends RFC 0001 §5.1, where creating a human Member was an Admin act for each person. The Admin's step-up now authorises the rule, and the rule is the audited authority act.

### The first Admin and lockout

- The bootstrap credential (`YG_BOOTSTRAP_TOKEN`, held in memory and never stored) runs `yg admin bootstrap` on the server host. It configures at least one Login Provider and names the first Admin by provider identity, then prints a single-use enrolment link that expires in about 15 minutes. The named person logs in, opens the link and enrols two hardware keys (below).
- After that, the bootstrap credential can recover Admin access through `yg admin recover`, run on the server host with a stated reason. It revokes a named Admin's factors, or names a new Admin by provider identity together with a revocable admission record for that exact identity, and prints a fresh single-use enrolment link. Recovery of an existing Admin, when it takes effect, also revokes their web sessions, CLI and Personal Agent tokens, active Break-glass Sessions and pending ceremonies, as Member recovery does. If no configured Login Provider can authenticate the recovery identity, the same act may configure a replacement provider for that identity, under the same audit, notices and delay; Admin access returns only after a verified login through it and enrolment of two hardware keys. Every use is audited and sends a notice to every Admin.
- The server cannot tell a lost key from a working one, so timing depends only on what it can see. If some Admin other than the recovery's target is unsuspended and has completed an Admin step-up within the last 30 days, the recovery takes effect after 24 hours, and any such Admin may cancel it with step-up in that time. A cancel is audited and noticed like the recovery, and after one cancel, a repeat recovery for the same target can no longer be cancelled; it only waits the 24 hours. In every other case, and while the restore gate is closed, recovery takes effect at once. The bootstrap credential keeps its other RFC 0001 roles unchanged.
- A suspended Admin (admission lapsed, or Liveness Window passed) keeps the role on record but can perform no Admin act and does not count as active.

### Re-keying a Login Provider

An IdP can change its issuer for everyone at once (a custom domain, a tenant merge, a move to another IdP). An Admin then re-keys the provider, with a hardware-key step-up, a reason, audit and a notice to every Admin. The act maps the old issuer to the new one, keeping each subject where the tenant preserves it, or translating it through a stable claim the Admin declares. A re-keyed identity holds no admission until its person completes a fresh login through the new issuer and touches a passkey already enrolled to that Member, which is the self-verification. Members with no surviving passkey use recovery. A login through the new issuer that matches a re-keyed identity never creates a second Member. SCIM directory records, suspensions included, are re-keyed in the same act; a record whose new identifier cannot be mapped keeps its identity ineligible for admission until an Admin reconciles it.

### Step-up

- Every step-up act from the CLI or REST is a **browser hand-off**. The client creates a pending act and gets a URL on Yggdrasil's origin. The page shows the exact act: for a promote, the Memory, its Audience and the shared head it expects to displace. The pending act is bound to the human Member and credential that created it and to its exact arguments. The page completes it only for that same Member: they touch one of their own eligible passkeys with user verification, the server re-checks the initiating credential and the Member's authority, completes that act and consumes the challenge once. A device grant is the one ceremony whose subject comes from the approver. The CLI polls for the outcome. Ceremony state stays on the server.
- TOTP is no longer a factor: an agent that can see the screen or read the seed can pass it. Direct CTAP2 from the CLI is not offered now and can be added later without changing the rest.
- **Members** may use any passkey with user verification, synced ones included. **Admin** step-ups need an attested, non-synced hardware key, and every Admin enrols two on separate authenticators: each registration excludes the Admin's existing credential ids, so one device cannot hold both. Admin step-ups cover widening, Break-glass Sessions, issuing Service Agent tokens, writing Member Admission Rules, moving Forge Links and lockout recovery. A Team Maintainer's widenings (adding a Member to an unsynced Team that holds Restricted grants, clearing a local revoke they made) and a Member's own Forge Link take a Member-grade passkey.
- **Enrolling a passkey.** A Member's first passkey, and any passkey enrolled while they hold none, needs a fresh authentication at the Login Provider. The request asks for one (`prompt=login`, `max_age=0`, sent by pushed authorization request where the IdP supports it), but the server decides from what comes back. The ceremony is bound at creation to the stable login identity expected to authenticate, and it refuses enrolment unless the callback's identity equals that one, the ID token's `nonce` is the one bound to this enrolment ceremony and its `auth_time` is present and no earlier than the ceremony's start, less a few seconds of clock skew, and the callback arrives within the ceremony's 10-minute lifetime. An Admin sets per provider whether the ID token must also show the IdP's own multi-factor authentication (`amr` or `acr`); for a provider so configured, missing evidence refuses the enrolment. An ambient SSO session is never enough. GitHub's OAuth cannot force a fresh login, so on a GitHub login the first enrolment needs either an Admin-issued enrolment link or a deployment that accepts that risk explicitly. Every enrolment and removal is audited and notifies the Member through every other channel Yggdrasil has for them. An Admin may restrict Member passkeys to an allow-list of authenticator models. With the allow-list on, enrolment needs trusted attestation that binds the credential to an allowed model; a self-attested or missing attestation is refused, since an AAGUID alone can be claimed by any software authenticator. Without the allow-list, software authenticators are accepted.
- A Member adds and removes their own passkeys by stepping up with one they still hold, and can never remove their last one: a replacement is registered first. Once a Member has enrolled, holding no passkey opens only recovery or the restore path, never the first-passkey enrolment. An Admin's hardware keys are different: the first two are enrolled inside the stepped-up ceremony that made them Admin (appointment, bootstrap or recovery). An appointment is pending until the appointee accepts it in their own session with their Member passkey and registers both keys, and the role activates only when the second registration succeeds; any later change to an Admin's key set needs a step-up with one of their existing hardware keys, and ordinary removal can never take the set below two. A Member who has lost every passkey is recovered by an Admin acting with step-up, since every human Member holds the promote right by default (RFC 0001 §5.1). Recovery is a replacement. In one transaction it revokes the Member's old passkeys, web sessions, CLI tokens, Personal Agent tokens, pending approvals and every device grant they approved that has not been redeemed, and puts the Member into a recovery state, in which the ordinary first-passkey path is closed. It issues a single-use enrolment link bound to that Member and to a recovery generation that every restart of recovery advances, so an earlier link stops working, which also needs the fresh provider authentication above and is consumed in the same transaction that registers the replacement key. Each agent that should regain access needs a new device grant approved from the recovered account. Recovery redeems only through an identity the Member verified themselves. If none is left, it may go through an Admin-attached identity only as a Break-glass-grade act: a reason, audit, and notices to the Member and to every tier ADR 0008 names for each Restricted repo the recovered account will be able to reach. If the account is still suspended when the key is registered, those notices are recorded when it is reactivated. A lost last Admin key is lockout, handled above.

### Credentials

Every credential is a `member_tokens` row of one of four kinds, so one hash, expiry, revoke and audit path, and one restore-gate rule, cover them all.

| Kind | How it is issued | Lifetime | Accepted on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Web session | OIDC login in the browser, then a passkey touch with user verification by a passkey enrolled to that Member (an OIDC login alone yields only a session that can enrol a first passkey); carried in a `__Host-` cookie, `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict`, with CSRF protection | 30 min idle, 8 h absolute; the id is rotated on login and on step-up | the web GUI |
| Human CLI | `yg login`, a device grant Yggdrasil hosts (RFC 8628) | about 12 h, no refresh | REST and CLI; refused over MCP |
| Personal Agent | `yg mcp login`, the same device grant requesting an agent token | 90 days by default; the maximum is set by an Admin | MCP and REST, as that agent Member |
| Service Agent | an Admin acting with step-up | set by the Admin | MCP and REST, under its reader ceiling (ADR 0008) |

- **Device-grant approval is a step-up ceremony.** The human opens the code page in the web GUI, where they are logged in, and types the user code; a code prefilled from a link is never accepted. The page shows the token kind in plain words ("human CLI session, not for agents" or "Personal Agent token for <name>, bound to you") and the source address and coarse location the server observed for the request, never values the client claims, with a warning when they differ from the approving browser's. Approving takes a passkey touch. User codes expire after 10 minutes and are rate-limited.
- **Every grant is told.** Each approval is audited and notifies the approving Member through their other channels, naming the token kind, the observed source and when it was approved, so a Member tricked into approving someone else's request learns of it at once. The alert carries a revoke action keyed to the grant, serialised with redemption, so it cancels a grant not yet redeemed or revokes the token if redemption came first. Each Member also has a list of their Personal Agents and CLI sessions with one-touch revoke. An Admin may cap Personal Agents per Member and restrict the networks a grant may come from.
- **A Personal Agent is bound by the approver.** The Member who approves the grant becomes its principal, and the binding never changes. Binding a Personal Agent to oneself is not a widening under ADR 0008: the agent never sees more than its principal and acts for no other human, so no one new can see any repo. The approval alert below still goes out.
- **Creating and reissuing are different grants.** A grant either creates a new Personal Agent or reissues a token for an existing one. For a reissue, the principal picks the agent from their own list, which shows its immutable id and any publishing right an Admin gave it, and approval and redemption are bound to that id; a display name is never used to find it. A reissue replaces: redeeming it revokes the agent's previous tokens in the same transaction, while an abandoned or expired reissue leaves the current token usable. Each approved reissue is bound to the agent's current credential generation, which every revocation and every replacement advances in the same transaction that revokes tokens; redemption checks it, so a revoke made after approval also kills the pending reissue.
- A human CLI approval is bound to the web session that approved it; logout of that session, including an IdP back-channel logout, cancels it if unredeemed and revokes its token if redemption came first.
- Suspension cancels every approved, unredeemed device grant of that Member, so reinstatement needs fresh approval. Redemption of a device grant is serialised with recovery and suspension, so a concurrent redemption either fails or issues a token that the recovery or suspension then revokes. There is no path that hands a Personal Agent token to a human to paste; a remote sandbox uses the same grant, because the human types the code into their own browser.
- `yg mcp` and the MCP endpoint accept only agent tokens. A leaked human CLI token still cannot publish, because every publishing act needs its own step-up.
- Web sessions and human CLI tokens end on logout, revoke, expiry, the Member's admission lapsing, an IdP back-channel logout where the IdP sends one, or a restore, which revokes all of them and makes everyone log in again.

### Offboarding

- Every human Member, Admins included, must log in successfully within the **Liveness Window**: 7 days by default, set by an Admin. Otherwise their Membership is suspended, and so is every Personal Agent bound to them, until they log in again and an admission still holds. Suspension is a narrowing and needs no step-up. Members admitted through a Forge Namespace rule are also reconfirmed by Forge sync, which usually notices first.
- An IdP may also push deprovisioning through an optional SCIM endpoint. `active=false` suspends the Member and their Personal Agents. A SCIM user is matched to a login identity by the provider's stable account identifier, configured per provider (for Okta, the user's `externalId` set to the OIDC `sub`), never by `userName` or email. Creates and updates are accepted as inert directory records keyed by that identifier, because IdPs provision before they deprovision; a record creates no Member and grants nothing, which stays with admission. `active=false` on a record sticks: a later login through that identity is suspended on arrival. `active=true` only clears that record's flag, and is audited. Suspensions are tracked by their source (each SCIM record, liveness, admission), and the Member and their Personal Agents resume only at a login through an identity with an admission that holds while no suspension from any source remains. Where the IdP cannot send it, an Admin clears the flag with step-up. Admins are alerted only when a record's identifier conflicts with an existing login identity. Its bearer token is a Service-Agent-like credential an Admin issues with step-up, bound to one configured provider and tenant and accepted only on SCIM routes. The scope comes from the credential, never from the request, and directory records are keyed by that scope plus the stable account identifier.

### Forge Links

- A **Forge Link** binds a Member to a Forge account, keyed by the Forge and its numeric user id, never by login, so a renamed or reclaimed login cannot inherit it. It is never inferred from Contributor merges or email. Setting one is a widening (ADR 0008), made in one of two ways:
  - **By the Member.** They complete that Forge's OAuth, then confirm on a Yggdrasil page that names the account (numeric id and login) and the synced Teams it would add, with a passkey step-up and a one-line reason. The link stays pending until sync holds fresh membership and grant data for the Teams it affects, so the confirmation always shows the real effect. It is audited, and its widening notices for the Restricted repos those Teams reach are recorded durably when it activates. Logging in through a Forge for the first time proposes the link the same way; it is not made silently.
  - **By an Admin**, with step-up, a reason, audit and a notice.
- An account the Forge marks as a bot or machine account is never linked.
- **SSO match.** Where a Forge Namespace enforces SAML SSO and its external identities are visible to the service credential (GitHub's `externalIdentities`, GitLab's `group_saml_identity`), an Admin may configure, with step-up, an SSO match for that Forge connection. It names the Login Provider, which must be the IdP tenant behind the Namespace's SAML, and the ID-token claim that must equal the Forge's SAML `nameId` or SCIM `externalId`. That claim's value is captured from a fresh login and stored on the login identity, with the time it was last asserted, so later checks compare stored IdP-asserted values, never a live email. Every validated login through that identity replaces the stored value, or clears it when the claim is absent. The value counts only while that exact login identity is inside its Liveness Window, and a missing, expired or mismatched value suspends the derived access; a Forge poll refreshes only the Forge side of the match. Configuring the Forge's SAML app to send the IdP's immutable user id as `nameId` is recommended, so the match is not email-based. With a match configured, the account's external identity must equal the Member's stored value, or the self-link is refused and only an Admin can set it. Without a match configured, the check is off and self-links follow the step-up-and-notice path above. With one, sync re-checks the match on every poll: a mismatch suspends the access the link derives from that Namespace, and a match that cannot be verified lapses at the maximum age like any other Forge-derived state. An Admin-set link is a separate audited decision and is exempt.
- A Member may hold many Forge Links; their synced Team memberships are the union across them. A Forge account links to at most one Member. A second Member who proves control of a linked account is refused and told to ask an Admin. An Admin may move the link with step-up, which narrows the old holder and widens the new one; both are told.
- A login identity at a Forge and a Forge Link to the same account are separate records. Moving the Forge Link also disables the old holder's login identity for that account and ends its sessions, so whoever now controls the account cannot enter the old holder's Membership. The old holder keeps any other login identity. If the moved account was their only one, the move waits until the old holder has verified and confirmed a replacement login identity themselves; if they cannot, the move suspends the old Membership and its Personal Agents in the same transaction, and they return through recovery. Making that account a login identity of the new holder is a separate Admin act.
- Sync reconfirms that a linked account still exists from the org and team listings it already reads, not by a call per account. A linked account that appears in no listing (a personal account used only for contributions) is reconfirmed by a lookup of its stable id once a day, and its existence has a maximum age of two days; that lookup is part of the poller's rate budget. A deleted account's link lapses. Team membership and grants keep their own freshness rules either way.
- A Member is linked to Contributors only through their Forge Links (`identities.kind = forge_account`), never through a commit email, which anyone can write into git.

### Forge sync

- **Credential.** Each Forge connection syncs with its own service credential, never a person's:
  - GitHub: a GitHub App installation with Members read, Metadata and Contents read, whose user OAuth also serves the Forge Link step;
  - GitLab: a group access token or service account at the top-level group, with `read_api`;
  - Forgejo and Codeberg: a dedicated bot account in the org with a read-scoped token.

  Only teams the credential can see are synced. The credential cannot list what it cannot see, so an Admin may declare the Forge team ids they expect to sync. The admin console flags any expected or previously seen team that sync cannot verify, and shows inventory coverage as unknown where the credential cannot prove its listing is complete. Replacing a connection's credential is an Admin act with step-up; if the new credential sees more, the change is a widening and sends a notice.
- **Who a synced Team contains.** A Member is in a synced Team when one of their Forge Links is an *active* member of the Forge team in the Forge's own effective view (GitHub's listing including child teams; GitLab's `/members/all`, including inherited and shared-group members; Forgejo's flat teams), with a role that carries code read: GitHub member or above, GitLab Reporter or above, Forgejo a team with Code read. Pending invitations and Guest, Planner and Minimal Access roles never count. This is the same filter ADR 0008 applies to Forge-derived repo grants.
- **Unlinked accounts.** A Forge account in a team with no linked Member is held as an unclaimed entry and takes effect at the first sync after a Member links it. Bot and machine accounts never become Members, since only a human links an account. Agents get Teams from their principal (Personal Agents) or from an Admin (Service Agents).
- **Freshness.** Forge-derived state is re-polled every 5 minutes and lapses when not reconfirmed within 30 minutes. That covers Team memberships, repo grants, repo visibility on the Forge, Forge Links and Forge Namespace admission rules. An Admin may set the interval between 5 and 60 minutes and the maximum age between 3× the interval and 24 hours. Raising the maximum age never revives anything already lapsed, and an observation keeps the expiry it was given; the longer age applies from the next successful reconfirmation. Webhooks, where a Forge offers them, only trigger an early re-poll of the affected team or repo; they are never applied as deltas. Freshness is tracked per item, and a conditional request answered "not modified" counts as reconfirmation. The poller is budget-aware: it measures what a full cycle costs against the Forge's rate budget, alerts Admins when a cycle overruns its interval, and refuses an interval it has measured it cannot meet. Lapse still happens at the maximum age, whether the cause is an outage or an exhausted rate budget; Admins are alerted before it, once an item is past half its maximum age, and again when it happens. Explicit Admin grants and memberships of local Teams never lapse.
- **Converting a local Team to a synced one** replaces its membership with the Forge's and brings in the Forge team's repo grants. The conversion compares each Member's ordinary view before and after, excluding any Break-glass Session, including grants first discovered by sync, and every Restricted repo it newly exposes to anyone is a widening attributed to the conversion, with a notice.
- **Roles.** No Forge role maps to a Yggdrasil role. Forge org owners do not become Admins, and Forge team maintainers do not become Team Maintainers; an Admin appoints both with step-up. Forge roles feed only the code-read filter.

### Restore

The new tables (login identities, Member Admission Rules, pending logins, Forge Links, passkeys and their ceremony state, and every credential kind) are authority revisions under the restore gate (RFC 0001 §5.1). On restore, every web session and human CLI token is revoked, and every pending ceremony (device grants approved or not, enrolment links, pending step-up acts) is invalidated: each is bound to the deployment's generation, which is checked at approval and at redemption and changes when the gate closes. Every Forge-derived membership, grant and admission is treated as unconfirmed until the next successful sync, so it fails closed. A Forge Link is Yggdrasil's own decision, which a poll cannot confirm: its holder comes from replaying the authority-revision log, and where the log cannot prove the latest binding, the link stays disabled until a fresh stepped-up binding, with its widening notices. Members now issue their own Personal Agent tokens and passkeys, so no operator can confirm those by hand. The gate reopens only after they are rebuilt mechanically: by replaying their revocations, enrolments and removals from the authority-revision log, or, where the log cannot prove completeness, by revoking every Personal Agent token and every Member passkey. SCIM suspensions, including those on directory records with no Member yet, are replayed the same way; where they cannot be, every Member with a login identity in that SCIM scope is suspended across all their identities and credentials, with their Personal Agents, and login through that provider stays closed for identities with no Member yet, until its suspension state is reconciled with the IdP: by an Admin, or, when that suspends every Admin, by the bootstrap credential on the server host, audited and noticed, against current IdP evidence. Identities whose status stays unresolved remain suspended, and Admins then sign in normally. Members then re-enrol under the fresh-login rule, and Admins through `yg admin recover`.

## Considered options

- **The Forge as the only IdP.** The login is the link, which is simple. But a personal GitHub account outlives employment, so there is no offboarding gate, and a deployment with several Forges needs a login per Forge.
- **An external broker** (Dex, Keycloak, Authentik). One OIDC client inside Yggdrasil, but another service for every self-hosted deployment, and its `sub` wraps the upstream identity so a Forge Link still needs a separate proof.
- **Invitation-only or approval-only Membership.** Strictest and closest to RFC 0001 as written, but an Admin acts for every hire, and offboarding is never automatic.
- **Declaring the first Admin in the deploy config.** No token step, but a config edit would mint an Admin silently.
- **IdP device grants exchanged for Yggdrasil tokens.** Per-provider quirks, Forgejo has none, and the approval happens at the IdP, where Yggdrasil cannot make it a step-up ceremony that names the token kind.
- **Admin-issued or pasted Personal Agent tokens.** An Admin acting for every agent does not scale, and pasting puts a bearer secret on the clipboard; the device grant puts it straight into the agent's environment.
- **TOTP or direct CTAP2 for CLI step-up.** TOTP is the factor most exposed to an agent, and neither shows the human the act being approved.
- **Hardware keys for Team Maintainers' widenings too.** It would put every Restricted widening behind hardware, at one key per Team Maintainer. Widening notices already make an abuse visible, and the cost was judged not worth it.
- **Hardware keys for everyone, or for no one.** For everyone works against admission by rule; for no one leaves widening and Break-glass behind a synced passkey an agent with computer use might drive.
- **A separate session store, or stateless cookies.** A second store doubles the revoke and restore story; stateless cookies cannot be revoked at all.
- **Hourly polling with a day's maximum age.** Cheap and outage-tolerant, but a removed employee keeps Restricted access for up to an hour, and a stale grant lingers for a day.
- **Direct membership only, or excluding GitLab shared groups.** Both contradict what people see on their Forge, and direct-only contradicts expanding nested teams.
- **An Admin's personal token for sync.** Quicker setup, but what everyone sees would then depend on one person's access, secret teams included.
- **Latest proof wins for a Forge Link.** Smoother for a rebuilt Member, but a borrowed Forge session would silently move someone's Team access.
- **Shorter agent tokens instead of liveness.** A daily passkey ceremony per agent, and the gap still lasts a day.
- **Mapping Forge roles to Yggdrasil roles.** Less setup, but Forge administration would then mint Yggdrasil authority without step-up or a notice.

## Consequences

- The `members` table RFC 0001 §2 sketched is finally needed, together with login identities, Member Admission Rules, pending logins, Forge Links, passkeys and a `kind` column on `member_tokens`.
- Yggdrasil implements an RFC 8628 device-authorization endpoint, a WebAuthn relying party (`webauthn-rs`, with attestation for Admin keys), an OIDC client and a GitHub OAuth adapter, and optionally a SCIM endpoint.
- Every step-up act needs a browser, and every Admin needs two hardware keys.
- **Accepted residual risk.** A Member's passkey may be synced or software-based, so an agent with computer use on a machine where that passkey unlocks without a fresh biometric could approve the Member's own device grants and step-ups. The same holds for a Team Maintainer's widenings, which take a Member-grade passkey; their widening notices make an abuse visible after the fact. Admin acts are protected by hardware keys, and an Admin may require an authenticator allow-list for Members; otherwise this risk is accepted, not prevented.
- Onboarding sends widening notices: each Member's first Forge Link reaches the Restricted repos their synced Teams hold. ADR 0008 lets those notices be batched, never dropped.
- IdP-admitted humans must log in at least once per Liveness Window, or their agents stop.
